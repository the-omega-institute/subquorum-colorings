# A balanced connected component need not allow strand cancellation

8 October 2026. This continues the [directed donor relay](DIRECTED_STRAND_RELAY.md)
and [one-color terminal criterion](TERMINAL_PRUNING_AND_COLOR_COMPENSATION.md).
We prove a pairing-independent cut obstruction and realize it on physical
rectangles of arbitrary length. It concerns reconnection of auxiliary strands,
not the truth of the grid inequality or the availability of aggregate charge.

## 1. Directed cut bounds that hold for every maximum pairing

Fix a feasible pair T,M, a deficient tile region D, and any maximum adjacent
auxiliary pairing in each tile. As before, X means L or P; S is an unpaired
positive-capacity endpoint. Give a P terminal the tile ownership of its inside
neighbor, but retain the checkerboard color of the actual outside endpoint.
Different outside endpoints are distinct terminals, even in the same outside
tile. For U contained in D, include all terminals owned by its tiles.

Orient every open strand from its black end to its white end. Physical
matching edges then point black to white, and auxiliary edges white to black.
Let b_out(U) count residual matching edges between U and D minus U whose
black endpoint is in U. Let b_in(U) count those whose white endpoint is in U.
Ports at the external boundary of D are included with their owners and are
not additional edges of this cut. Auxiliary edges never cross a tile cut.

Let D_b(U) count XX strands whose black X end is owned by U, and N_b(U)
count SS strands whose black S end is in U. Define the white counterparts
by the white end. All terminal counts below are invariant under changes of
maximum pairing, including the same-color S slide at residual degree three.

**Theorem 1 (directed terminal cut bounds).** For every maximum pairing,

    D_b(U) >= max(0, X_b(U)-S_w(U)-b_out(U)),
    D_w(U) >= max(0, X_w(U)-S_b(U)-b_in(U)),
    N_b(U) >= max(0, S_b(U)-X_w(U)-b_out(U)),
    N_w(U) >= max(0, S_w(U)-X_b(U)-b_in(U)).             (1)

**Proof.** Follow the strand from each black X terminal in U. It either
ends at a white X terminal, giving one of the D_b(U) strands, or ends at
S. At most S_w(U) of the latter end in U. Every remaining XS strand exits
the cut and uses an outgoing physical matching edge. Different strands
have disjoint physical endpoints and matching edges, so their number is
at most b_out(U). This proves the first inequality. Starting from a white
X terminal and traversing backwards proves the second. Interchange X and S
to obtain the last two. A strand may leave and reenter U many times; its
first exit still supplies a distinct cut edge. Cycles use no terminal and
cannot invalidate the bound. QED.

For pairwise tile-disjoint U_i, the black-end ownership gives the simultaneous
bound

    n_XX >= sum_i max(0, X_b(U_i)-S_w(U_i)-b_out(U_i)).  (2)

The corresponding all-white bound and either fixed-color SS bound also hold.
Each strand has exactly one black end, so (2) counts it at most once. Do not
add black and white bounds as separate budgets: they may certify the very
same strand. Overlapping U_i likewise need an explicit ownership argument.
Reserves c are a separate ledger and are not generated by these cut bounds.

These statements impose a necessary capacity condition on any proposed
complete auxiliary cancellation. No converse or general max-flow theorem is
asserted: sufficient routing must also satisfy the physical tile pairings.

## 2. An explicit arbitrary-length physical obstruction

For each integer k>=0, take a 6-by-(8+2k) rectangle, T empty, with the following
matching. Coordinates start at zero. Set z=4+2k.

Three left arms and their internal leaf edges are

    (2,2)--(1,2),  (3,2)--(3,1),  (3,3)--(4,3),
    (0,2)--(0,3),  (2,0)--(2,1),  (5,2)--(5,3).

The upper bridge corridor and its lower internal edges are

    (2,3+2j)--(2,4+2j),           0<=j<=k,
    (3,4+2j)--(3,5+2j),           0<=j<k.

The three right port arms are

    (2,z+1)--(1,z+1),
    (3,z)--(4,z),
    (3,z+1)--(3,z+2).

Let D consist of the left junction tile (tile row 1,column 1), the right
junction (1,k+2), the k intervening tiles (1,j), 2<=j<=k+1, and the three
left leaf tiles (0,1),(1,0),(2,1). The outside ends of the three right arms
are the only P terminals. Include no outside port tile in D.

**Theorem 2 (balanced connected obstruction).** This is a feasible physical
configuration for every k. The regional residual incidence graph is connected,
contains both colors of X terminals, and satisfies

    p=3, a=0, u=3, c=0, H=3,
    rho(D)=-3/2, epsilon(D)=0.

Nevertheless every maximum auxiliary pairing gives exactly

    n_PP=1, n_SS=1, n_PS=1,

with every other open-strand count zero and no cycle. Thus no sequence of
maximum auxiliary switches can eliminate its PP debt against its SS donor.
The distance between the two junction tiles is k+1.

**Proof.** All listed edges are unit grid edges with disjoint endpoints,
inside the rectangle. Since T is empty, this proves feasibility. Each left
leaf has one internal matching edge, r=1,d=1 and one S end. Each corridor
tile has one lower internal edge and two upper residual endpoints, hence
r=1,d=2, a unique upper auxiliary pair, and no reserve. Each junction has
r=2,d=4, no S end or reserve, and exactly two maximum auxiliary pairings.
The residual tile graph inside D is a tree, with the bridge corridor its only
connection between left and right. The degree sum is 11+2k and capacity sum
7+k, giving rho=-3/2. There are three ports and three S ends, so H=3,c=0
and epsilon=0. These computations hold for every pairing.

Take U to be the right junction tile alone, with its three owned P terminals.
Its colors are P_b=2,P_w=1. There is no S in U. Its one corridor cut edge
has its black endpoint in U, so b_out=1,b_in=0. Both debt bounds in (1)
give n_PP>=1. There are only three P ends, so n_PP<=1. With three S ends,
the exact endpoint counts force one SS and one PS. The residual tile graph
is a tree and has no parallel edges, so an auxiliary cycle is impossible.

Equivalently, each junction pairs two of its three local arms together and
connects the third to the corridor. The left local arms all end at S; the
right local arms all end at P. Choices therefore change identities without
changing the three strand types. There are exactly four maximum decompositions,
independent of k. The lower bound holds at every such decomposition and
therefore after every finite switch sequence. QED.

On the left side, choose U to consist of its junction and three leaf tiles.
There are S_b=1,S_w=2 and no X, with b_out=0,b_in=1. The SS bounds certify
its same one donor. The black and white debt bounds also certify the same PP;
adding them would falsely report two units of debt in this very fixture.

## 3. A bent bridge has the same obstruction

The obstruction also has a genuinely bent physical version. For k>=1, take
an 8-by-(8+2k) rectangle, z=4+2k, and the following matching, with T empty:

    (4,2)--(3,2), (5,2)--(5,1), (5,3)--(6,3),
    (2,2)--(2,3), (4,0)--(4,1), (7,2)--(7,3),
    (4,3+2j)--(4,4+2j),                 0<=j<=k,
    (5,4+2j)--(5,5+2j),                 0<=j<=k,
    (4,z+1)--(3,z+1),
    (2,z)--(1,z), (2,z+1)--(2,z+2), (3,z)--(3,z-1).

Its regional tiles are (2,j), 1<=j<=k+2, and (1,1),(2,0),(3,1),(1,k+2).
The left junction is (2,1), the right junction (1,k+2). Tile (2,k+2) is
the bend: its upper corners are its two residual endpoints, paired uniquely,
and its lower corners form an internal matching edge. The same disjoint-edge
feasibility and local capacity proof applies. The residual tile graph is still
a tree with a single corridor, and the right junction has two black and one
white ports, no S, and one outgoing cut edge. Thus every maximum pairing
again has one PP, one SS and one PS, with rho=-3/2,epsilon=0,c=0.
The restriction k>=1 keeps the right western port outside the left S tile.
Its whole-grid objective is 12+2k, below |V|/2=32+8k. A turn does not remove
the cut obstruction; this conclusion follows from the same all-size proof.

## 4. Precisely which proposed rule fails

The fixture refutes the assertion that an SS donor and an XX debt in one
connected component with nonpositive shortage can always be canceled by
changing maximum auxiliary pairings. Even exact budget balance, no reserves,
no cycles, arbitrary corridor length and capacity-two junctions do not suffice.
Undirected connectivity does not imply the directed relay hypothesis.
At the left junction, switching SS against PS sends SS onto the PS's S arm,
which stays on the left; the PP on the right remains unreachable this way.

The aggregate accounting rule remains valid: the one SS pays the one PP in
epsilon=n_XX-n_SS-c. This region already supplies exactly p/2 units of
negative charge. Physical strand cancellation is a sufficient certificate,
not a necessary condition for aggregate compensation. The fixture does not
refute coefficient one: on the entire rectangle |T|+|M|=10+2k and
|V|/2=24+6k. It does not improve the universal 5/6 coefficient either.

For continued mixed-color work, first distinguish a unique accounting
allocation from a realizable sequence of auxiliary switches. Cut bounds
can certify mandatory debts and donors despite numerical balance. A proof
using switches must accommodate these cuts, or allow other physical matching
transformations. Cross-component aggregate allocation still needs its own
geometric ownership proof, with saturated/inter-patch exports retained.

Run `python3 develop/check_mixed_color_cuts.py --output output/mixed-color-cut-controls.json`.
The control checks actual coordinates, both feasibility implementations,
every maximum pairing of the family and every tile cut of selected small
feasible regions. It also checks fixed-color disjoint ownership and the
explicit invalid double counting. Finite controls corroborate the all-size
proofs; they are not a proof by enumeration. No manuscript or Lean source
is changed.

Archived controls pass on 25,479 feasible pairs, 128,363 deficient regions,
129,259 full maximum-pairing decompositions and 319,383 directed cuts, including
fixed-color complementary and singleton ownership checks. The physical family
is checked at k=0..40 in its straight version and k=1..40 in its bent version,
in all eight rectangle symmetries, giving 648 coordinate controls and all
2,592 of their maximum-pairing decompositions. Both direct
coordinate and bit-mask feasibility checks pass. Source hashes accompany the
report; these new controls run independently of the existing shared CI.
