# Compensation in a forest of shrinking bands

30 September 2026. Continuation of [pressure-band nonpositivity](PRESSURE_BAND_BRANCHING.md)
and [neutral transport](COMPENSATION_TRANSPORT.md). This is a written proof
for a specified geometric class, not a proof of the unrestricted grid conjecture.
The manuscript supplied for the arXiv replacement is preserved.

## 1. The ledger and the new geometric condition

Fix an aligned 2-by-2 tiling of a feasible even-by-even rectangular grid.
Write T for selected vertices, P for matching endpoints and B for blanks.
For deficient tiles put rho(Q)=deg_Gamma(Q)/2-r_Q, and put rho(Q)=0 for
saturated tiles. The exact identity is

    q=|T|+|M|-mn/2=sum_Q rho(Q).

A horizontal pressure band S consists of w tiles in one tile row. Immediately
above every upper vertex is P. Its left cap is `TP/BT`, its right cap is
`PT/TB`, and each cap's P is matched upward. These are the hypotheses of
Theorem 1 of the pressure-band note. In particular

    rho(Q)<=0 for every Q in S,
    rho(S)=-Delta(S)+sigma(S)/2<=-1+sigma(S)/2.       (1)

Here sigma counts matching attachments to saturated tiles outside S and
Delta=(number of B-number of T)/2. All these attachments go downward.

There are two different rows in the following additional hypothesis:
the P row above S gives pressure; the **upper row within S** is also entirely
P. The second condition is new and is not automatic from neutrality.
The matching partners of these P vertices remain unrestricted.

## 2. Neutral bands with an upper row of P generate children

**Lemma 1 (successor intervals).** Suppose S is a pressure band, its upper
row is entirely P, and rho(S)=0. Orient an exit R when its matching endpoint
is the lower-right corner of its tile, and L when it is the lower-left.
Then:

1. The first exit is R and the last is L; hence at least one consecutive
   pair in the exit list has order R,L.
2. Between each such consecutive R,L pair, every intervening tile is full P.
3. If their tile indices are i<j, then j-i>=2. Their receiving saturated
   tiles are the caps of a pressure band one tile row below, with interval
   i+1,...,j-1. Its width lies between 1 and w-2.
4. The child intervals generated by all consecutive R,L pairs are disjoint.

The lemma permits lower-row selected vertices and capacity-two tiles.
It does not assert that a child is neutral, closed, or itself has an upper
row entirely P.

**Proof.** Since every tile has nonpositive charge, neutrality of S makes
each tile neutral. No tile with upper row PP is saturated: two lower T's
would each neighbor both an upper P and the other T. For a deficient tile
with t selected vertices, p endpoints and s saturated attachments,

    2rho(Q)=2t+p+s-4.                                  (2)

If t=1, its T is lower and its lower companion must be B, because T already
neighbors the P above it. Thus p=2 and s=0. If t=0, the two lower labels
are either PP, BP, PB or BB. A downward attachment requires a distinct
blank companion, and there is at most one per tile. Equation (2) leaves
exactly five neutral states:

| State | Tile labels | Saturated attachment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F | PP/PP | none |
| R | PP/BP | at lower-right P |
| L | PP/PB | at lower-left P |
| X | PP/BT | none |
| Y | PP/TB | none |

The lower band endpoints are B, as forced by the side caps. Therefore its
first state is X or R. An X ends in T, already adjacent to its upper P;
the next state's lower-left corner must be B, so that state is X or R.
The entire band cannot consist of X's, since its last lower corner is B.
The first exit is consequently R. Reflection gives the last exit L.

Consider consecutive exits R and L. An R ends in P. Before the next exit,
the available states are F,X,Y. A Y begins in T already neighboring its
upper P, so it cannot follow an occupied lower-right corner. If an X occurs,
it forces successive X's until an R, and cannot reach the next L. Thus only
F occurs in the intervening interval.

An R attachment enters the upper-right P of a saturated tile whose other
corners are `TP/BT`; an L similarly gives `PT/TB`. These two receiving tiles
cannot be adjacent: their lower facing T vertices would each have both its
own P neighbor and the other T as occupied neighbors. Therefore j-i>=2.
Every vertex directly above the upper row of the intervening child band
is a lower P of an F tile. The child has exactly the pressure and cap
conditions of (1). Its interval is strictly contained in that of S.

In an ordered exit list, two consecutive R,L pairs cannot share an exit:
a shared exit would have to have both orientations. Their open tile intervals
are disjoint. This proves all four assertions. QED.

The original single relay is the special word R F^(w-2) L. For example,
X^a R F^g L Y^b, with a,b>=0 and g>=1, still supplies a child of width g.
Its selected lower vertices are not forbidden and do not create extra demand.

## 3. A compensation theorem with explicit ownership

**Theorem 2 (branching interval compensation).** Let U_1,...,U_K be filled
source corridors of the boundary-aware compensation note, in the same tile
row and orientation. Their full horizontal tile spans, including the source
end tiles, are pairwise disjoint. Each has rho(U_i)=1 and supplies a first
pressure band S_i in the next row.

Starting from these K first bands, generate a tree of bands by the following
conditions, required for **every** generated band:

- A terminal band has sigma=0. Its labels and internal matching are otherwise
  arbitrary subject to feasibility.
- Every nonterminal band has rho=0 and an upper row entirely P. Generate all
  children given by Lemma 1.

Let D be the number of terminal bands in the resulting forest, and W the
deficient tiles outside all source and band regions. Then

    q <= K-D+rho(W).                                    (3)

In particular D>=K. If rho(W)<=0, then

    |T|+|M| <= mn/2-(D-K) <= mn/2.                      (4)

The matching may use both directions, cross the accounting regions, and have
capacity-two passages or routes revisiting a tile. The hypotheses concern
tile regions and labels, not simple residual paths.

**Proof.** Every nonterminal generates at least one child. Each child is in
the next tile row and has width at most its parent's width minus two. Thus
the recursion is finite. The stated dichotomy requires its leaves to be
terminal; widths one and two cannot satisfy the nonterminal alternative
by Lemma 1. Each of the K trees has at least one leaf, giving D>=K.

Sibling intervals are disjoint. All descendants have horizontal intervals
strictly inside their ancestors, so branches that separate never merge.
Two bands at different depths are in different tile rows. Distinct roots
have disjoint full column spans. Consequently all charged source and band
regions are pairwise tile-disjoint, and each terminal belongs to a unique
root. The receiving caps are saturated and have zero charge.

Each nonterminal contributes zero. For each terminal, (1) with sigma=0
gives rho<=-1. Sum the exact tile ledger once over the disjoint regions:

    q=sum_i rho(U_i)+sum_bands rho(S)+rho(W)
      <=K-D+rho(W).

This proves (3) and (4). Crossing residual edges cause no loss: their two
endpoint halves are already part of the exact ledger. No half-edge, tile,
terminal, or unit of source demand is inserted twice. QED.

**What improves.** On this class the coefficient of |M| increases from 5/6
to 1, and extra terminal branches give an additional deficit D-K. This is
not an improvement of the universal 5/6 constant. In the earlier unbounded
branching family, K=1, D=b and rho(W)=0; its exact q=1-b attains (3).

For source corridors at different tile rows, overlapping forward intervals,
rotated successors at bends, or a nonterminal of negative charge with sigma>0,
the theorem supplies no automatic ownership or continuation. Such regions
must not be silently inserted into this forest. In particular the hypothesis
rho(W)<=0 must be verified, rather than inferred from the selected chains.

## 3a. Allocation interpretation of the forest

The cross-component ownership in Theorem 2 can be stated with the interval
deficiency tool. Order all terminal bands from left to right, and for each
source root let `I_i` be the interval from its first to last terminal
descendant. Distinct roots in the construction have disjoint terminal
intervals, and every terminal band has capacity one. Thus every donor interval
`J` contains at least as many terminal capacity units as it contains complete
root windows `I_i`; the containment tests of
[the interval-deficiency note](INTERVAL_HALL_DEFICIENCY.md) hold and
`delta=0`. Internal successor intervals may be nested arbitrarily inside
`I_i` without changing this conclusion, because they are not additional
source demands and each terminal is counted once.

This gives a second proof of the no-reuse part of Theorem 2: the geometric
forest establishes disjoint leaf ownership, while interval Hall supplies the
cross-root allocation. In the existing multiple-root coordinate controls with
branch counts `(1,1)`, `(1,2)`, `(2,3,1)` and `(4,2,3,1)`, the interval
deficiency is exactly zero in all four cases. The coordinate remainder is still
kept in the ledger; zero allocation deficiency does not by itself assert that
the remainder is nonpositive for an arbitrary ambient configuration.

## 4. A neutral band need not generate a P-pressure successor

Here is a feasible 6-by-14 example outside Lemma 1. Rows and columns start
at zero. Its complete labels are:

```text
TPPPPPPPPPPPPT
BPPPPPPPPPPPPB
TPPPBTBTBPPPPT
BTBPTBPBTPPBTB
TBTPBTPTBBPTBT
BTBTBBTBTBTBTB
```

Use the filled source with k=5, so its frame T's are
{(0,0),(0,13),(2,0),(3,1),(2,13),(3,12)}. Add

    {(2,5),(3,4),(2,7),(3,8),
     (4,2),(5,3),(4,7),(5,6),(4,11),(5,10),
     (4,0),(5,1),(4,13),(5,12),(4,5),(5,8)}.

The matching consists of

    {((0,2i+1),(0,2i+2)): 0<=i<=5},
    {((1,2i),(1,2i+1)): 1<=i<=5},
    ((1,1),(2,1)), ((1,12),(2,12)),
    ((2,2),(2,3)), ((2,9),(3,9)), ((2,10),(2,11)),
    ((3,3),(4,3)), ((3,6),(4,6)), ((3,10),(4,10)).

These edges have disjoint endpoints. Each selected vertex has at most one
occupied neighbor, by direct inspection of the displayed rows. The source
has charge +1. The five inner tiles of row pair 2,3 are

    PP/BP,  BT/TB,  BT/PB,  BP/TP,  PP/PB.

The second is saturated; all others have zero charge. Thus the band has
rho=0, sigma=3 and Delta=3/2. Its ordered exits are R,L,L. Its only consecutive
R,L pair encloses one tile, but the row directly above that candidate child
is TB, not PP and not entirely occupied. There is no P-pressure successor
from an R,L pair in this example.

The enclosed bottom tile is `BT/BB`, with rho=-1. The other deficient bottom
tile is `BB/TB`, also with rho=-1. All other deficient nonsource tiles are
neutral. Hence

    |T|=22, |M|=19, mn/2=42, q=-1,
    residual component excesses: +1, 0, 0, 0, 0, -1, -1.

This refutes the proposed inference that **every** neutral pressure band
has a successor with a P pressure row. It does not refute coefficient one:
the target has one unit of spare capacity here. The missing upper-row-P
condition in Lemma 1 cannot simply be dropped from that inference.

develop/results/branching-interval-compensation-2026-09-30.json`.
The checker uses the existing coordinate feasibility, residual reconstruction

The enumerations are controls of the arguments above; they are not all-size
proofs or Lean formalizations.

After joint review, Lemma 1 and Theorem 2 can follow the boundary-aware lemma
after Proposition B.13. The 6-by-14 example should accompany their scope:
it makes the remaining mixed neutral states explicit. The submitted manuscript,
its general 5/6 theorem, and the unrestricted grid conjecture are preserved.

The next problem is to replace the upper-row-P dichotomy by a successor rule
that handles saturated diagonal tiles and half-unit deficits, then prove
ownership for overlapping intervals and turns. The SF_2 and Gamma attachments
do not currently supply these missing steps.
