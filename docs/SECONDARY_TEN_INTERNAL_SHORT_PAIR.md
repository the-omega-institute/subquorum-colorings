## Actual-owner coupling at incident depth ten

Fix any internal j and write a=a_j,s=s_j,w=w_j,C=s+w+13.
Suppose its actual E owner has incident depth ten:

    R=U(L,10,f), chart(r,c)->(a-11+r,C+1-c), f>=4 even.

Then its actual t owner T necessarily has the form

    T=U(M,4,g), chart(r,c)->(a-8+r,s+1-c),
    M,g even, M>=6,g>=4, and L>=w+M+14.                 (1)

This classifies T's orientation and incident leg, and bounds its breadth;
it does not classify g or assert sufficiency. In particular L>=w+20.
The former tight R breadth w+14 is impossible at incident ten for EVERY
far depth, not merely the sampled negative-marginal example.

Proof. Put F=C+1-L, the R far column. R occupies all far-column rows
a-11,...,a-11+f, including a-8. P_j's bar row a-8 occupies every column
s+1,...,C-1. Since L>=6, F<=C-5, so endpoint disjointness forces F<=s,
equivalently L>=w+14. Every R bar point at row a-10 and columns F,...,C
is an endpoint, including (a-10,s).

The actual t owner has an incident column at s ending at row a-4. If
its even incident depth were at least six, that column would include
(a-10,s), colliding with R. Hence its depth is four. A right depth-four
t owner has bar endpoint (a-8,s+1), colliding with P. It must be left,
in the displayed chart. Its bar row a-8 occupies columns G=s-M+1,...,s.
R contains (a-8,F); since F<=s, avoiding T requires F<G, hence F<=s-M.
Substitution yields L>=w+M+14. All comparisons concern complete original
endpoint sets, including bar rows zero and one. The old same-color
terminal argument ensures R and T are distinct actual originals.
