# Support-chain compensation and the remaining full-chain obstruction

20 October 2026. We reconstruct support-chain routing in the complete
U/rotor model and combine it with the two-slot and tight-gate witnesses.
The new routes use original regular-pair stopping tiles; the older
witnesses use original irregular-pair tiles. This distinction proves
simultaneous non-reuse, including across different source components.
The unrestricted mixed allocation and rectangular-grid equality remain open.

## 1. Assumptions, actual counts, and result

Keep the unchanged finite complete-source model: disjoint original
endpoint sets, all original matching edges and endpoints retained, every
original support outside their union P retained in final T, and each
final selected vertex having at most one occupied neighbor. U widths
are independently even and at least6; both heights are independently
even and at least4. All isometries and rotor phases are allowed.
Original source ownership and aligned two-by-two tiles are fixed.

Use the accepted exact source ledger and defect identity:

    mu := N+R-G = n0+R-n2,       sigma >= mu,
    2R = A+U0+Z0+H,             R is a nonnegative integer,
    H = L1+2L2bar+2L2mutual+L3.

Here mu is the allocation margin; sigma is the ambient coefficient-one
slack. A negative mu alone is not a negative ambient slack. A is the
number of activated original positive terminal singleton occurrences;
U0 counts unfilled original phase11-U triple caps. Keep z,s,t for actual
nonexceptional doubles with zero/one/two activated terminal occurrences,
e for actual exceptional doubles, and n0 for unmarked positives.
Nonexceptional means width6 at arbitrary allowed heights, or width8/10
with both heights4/6. Thus n2=z+s+t+e and A>=s+2t.

Retain r from WIDTH_SIX_TWO_GATE_INTERLEAVING.md: single-activation
nonexceptional doubles paid by a blank canonical tile or by a tight
longitudinal witness. Count each such source once. The accepted joint
assignment uses 2z+r distinct H slots, each carrying an original 2P0T pair.

Define two further counts from the actual fusion, without a search bound:

- J counts actual occupied marked gates whose original owner is a phase11
  U terminal, and whose associated original cap-to-tip chain has an
  unfilled triple cap or a nonfull regular receiver. All positive marked
  sides qualify, including one-mark sources and activated sides. A chain
  with every receiver full contributes zero to J.
- X counts original positive legs whose terminal singleton is unactivated
  but at least one original exterior regular-receiver selection is consumed
  by a foreign original endpoint. Count each leg once, regardless of how
  many of its exterior selections are consumed.

The common-pool theorem is

    U0+H >= 2z+r+J+X,                              (1)
    mu >= n0-e-floor((s-r-J-X)/2).                  (2)

The floor is signed integer floor, including when s-r-J-X is negative.
An equivalent sufficient joint-allocation condition is

    s+2e <= 2n0+r+J+X+1.                          (3)

This permits compensating unpaid doubles with routes generated by other
positive sources. Two additional routed units improve (2) by at least one.
No new credit is taken from a full tile already used in R.

## 2. A nonfull stopping receiver supplies a distinct L3 unit

Take any original regular 2P1T receiver Q. Write its adjacent endpoint
pair as p,q, its selection as t, and its original blank as b, with t
adjacent to p and b. Suppose t is consumed and Q is not full.
Then p,q,t belong to P, while b does not. Feasibility excludes b from T
as well: b sees the two endpoints q and t. Thus Q is an actual nonfull
mixed three-endpoint tile and supplies one L3 unit of H.

This fact uses original ownership. The pair remains with its original
regular owner; t has a different owner. The two-endpoint pair in this
stopping tile is a 2P1T profile, not a reclassified 2P0T profile.

Two routes with disjoint original endpoint pairs cannot stop in the
same nonfull tile: their four endpoints would make that tile full.
This elementary obstruction will give simultaneous injectivity.

## 3. Exterior positive chains stop or activate their own terminal

Normalize one positive leg to column1 with height a. Its exterior
regular receivers, including the bar-end receiver, are

    Q_j={2j,2j+1} x {0,1},              0<=j<a/2,
    original endpoints: (2j,1),(2j+1,1),
    original selection t_j=(2j,0), blank b_j=(2j+1,0).

For a leg counted by X, start at its first consumed t_j. If Q_j is
nonfull, Section2 supplies a stop. If Q_j is full, b_j is an endpoint.
The next mandatory selection t_(j+1)=(2j+2,0) now sees both its original
anchor(2j+2,1) and b_j. It cannot remain selected and must be consumed.

Iteration either stops at the first nonfull receiver or consumes the
terminal selection(a,0). The latter would activate the original terminal
singleton, contrary to the definition of X. Therefore every X leg has
a stopping L3 tile. No terminal activation is spent by this assignment.
Reflecting the normalized source proves the other side; all isometries
and independent heights transport the same argument.

Different positive legs have disjoint original receiver pairs. In
particular the left/right exterior bar-end tiles are distinct for w>=6.
Section2 makes their stopping tiles distinct.

## 4. Phase11 cap-to-tip chains stop or remain completely full

Normalize the phase11 owner as U(w,a,b)+(1,1), with the relevant left
stem in column2. Its cap Qcap={2,3}x{2,3} has original endpoints
(2,2),(2,3),(3,2) and missing corner m=(3,3). Its regular receivers are

    Q_j={2j,2j+1} x {2,3},              2<=j<=a/2,
    original endpoints: (2j,2),(2j+1,2),
    original selection t_j=(2j,3), blank b_j=(2j+1,3).

An unfilled cap provides its own U0 occurrence. If the cap is full,
m is an endpoint. The mandatory selection(4,3) sees m and its original
anchor(4,2), so it is consumed. At each full Q_j, the newly occupied
blank b_j forces the next mandatory selection to be consumed. A first
nonfull regular receiver is therefore exactly the stop of Section2.

For every gate counted by J, one of those two stopping alternatives
exists by definition: choose its unfilled cap, otherwise its first
nonfull regular receiver. A chain full all the way to its terminal
supplies no new unit here. A filled 3+1 contact is never paid again.

The gate identifies an original phase11 terminal tip. One original tip
cannot own two marked gates: it is a single physical point in one
physical canonical tile, and all positive canonical tiles are distinct.
Each original phase11 leg has its own triple cap and regular pairs.
Thus cap occurrences are not repeated, and different J routes have
disjoint original pairs when they stop in regular receivers.

The original regular pairs for J belong to negative phase11 U owners;
those for X belong to positive owners. They are disjoint across the two
route families as well. Section2 excludes sharing any stopping L3 tile.

## 5. Separate the new routes from every older witness

Every old two-slot/tight-gate H witness contains an original 2P0T pair.
Every new stopping L3 tile contains its routed original 2P1T pair.
If the pairs had different owners and the same tile, they would require
four endpoints, contradicting nonfullness. If they had the same owner,
that owner's fixed profile in this one tile would have to be both 2P0T
and 2P1T, also impossible. Hence none of the J/X stops uses an older
witness tile. This excludes even old forced L3 witnesses, not only the
blank-canonical L2bar witnesses.

New cap payments are distinct original U0 occurrences. The exact defect
identity justifies adding their units to H; labels alone would not.
In fact a cap triple cannot coincide with an old/new pair witness from
a different owner without demanding five endpoints; the same owner
cannot have both the triple and pair profiles in one physical tile.

Choose all old witnesses and all chain routes simultaneously. Their
within-family and cross-family exclusions provide exactly 2z+r+J+X
different actual U0/H units. This proves (1). The assignment does not
touch the A occurrences used for A>=s+2t, nor Z0, nor any full-tile C.

Combining (1) with the exact identity gives

    2R >= 2z+s+2t+r+J+X
       = 2(z+s+t)-(s-r-J-X).

Round once in the shared integer ledger, obtaining
R>=z+s+t-floor((s-r-J-X)/2). Substitution into mu=n0+R-n2
proves (2). Signed integer floor makes (3) exactly the nonnegative-bound
condition. No per-demand half unit has been rounded separately.

## 6. Component counts and sharper saturation

Every route stays in its actual endpoint component. At a consumed
selection, the foreign endpoint is adjacent to its persistent original
anchor. A filled cap or regular receiver similarly connects its owners.
Thus the accepted complete-source restriction to endpoint components
preserves all these routes and counts, and (2) applies to each component.

Let d_c=s_c-r_c-J_c-X_c. Summing the component bounds gives

    mu >= n0-e-sum_c floor(d_c/2).

If o components have odd d_c, this exceeds the single global bound (2)
by floor(o/2). This follows by writing each signed integer as
2floor(d_c/2)+(d_c mod2); the remainders are0 or1 even when d_c is
negative. It is additional component parity, with no extra physical
unit assigned. In particular two separate odd residuals need not share
one physical resource to gain an integer rounding unit.

In a failing nonexceptional R1 component, the accepted two-slot census
has n0=z=t=0,s=2,A=2,U0=Z0=H=0. Equation(1) now forces

    r=J=X=0.

So every marked phase11 gate in the entire component, including those
on one-mark positives, has a completely full cap-to-tip chain. Also no
positive leg with an unactivated terminal can have a consumed exterior
regular selection. Any consumed positive exterior receiver chain must reach one of the
two already activated positive terminals. The width-six long-side gap
>=6 and width8/10 transverse-gate restrictions still apply.

A stronger source-wide consequence holds whenever U0=H=0: EVERY
original endpoint-containing phase11-U receiver is full. Caps are full by U0=0. Each cap
then propagates down its complete regular leg; any nonfull stop would
be an L3 defect, contrary to H=0. Original 2P0T bar receivers are full
as well, because a nonfull such receiver would be L2bar or mixed L3.
This does not imply that such saturated finite fusions exist or are
impossible. Their coupled full chains remain the obstruction to the
unrestricted allocation proof.
