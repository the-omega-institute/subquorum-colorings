class of actual negative Vcorner claims. It does not require a positive

## 1. Complete-source hypotheses and statement

Use the finite complete original U/rotor model, unique original endpoint
ownership, unchanged matching and retention of every unconsumed original
support with its unique occupied anchor. Endpoint graphs are connected by
unit lattice edges. Any optional selected vertices have no endpoint neighbor.
Widths are even and at least six; independent legs are
even and at least four. All charts preserve the fixed aligned 2x2 tiles.

An actual normalized marked-side claim has its positive producer A, negative
gate consumer B, transverse source C and actual negative Vcorner X:

    A=U(w,L,K), A(r,c)=(r,c),
    B=U(10,b,e), B(r,c)=(b+3-r,c+1),
    C=U(V,h,f), C(r,c)=(c+1,h+2-r),
    X=U(b-2,a,4), X(r,c)=(c+2,r+3),
    b>=8, h>=8, 4<=a<=h-4, h<=w-4.

Endpoint disjointness is an explicit hypothesis. The claim is the actual
one at t=(3,2), with actual consumer X of y=(3,3). Additional sources
have arbitrary permitted dimensions and number. In particular this includes
gap-eight claims a=h-8 with maximal d=b-2, W=10. The earlier maximal-band
theorem already makes k=4 necessary when W=10.

**Theorem.** Completeness forces an original source

    Q=U(s,6,4), Q(r,c)=(b+2-c,9-r), 6<=s<=b-2.

Its phase is negative, with charge -1. No positive activation is assigned.
The physical tile

    J={b-2,b-1} x {4,5}

permanently contains precisely X's original irregular 2P0T pair
(b-2,4),(b-1,4). Its two other vertices remain unoccupied in EVERY complete
extension. Thus J contributes two units to 2L2bar in the existing exact
reserve identity, equivalently one unit to R.

The enclosure is also a rotated instance of the previously established
[lower-strip theorem, Section3](PERSISTENT_CORRIDOR_AND_STRIP_CAPACITY.md).
The new work here is the forced Q at arbitrary b,
the automatic assignment of Vcorner claims, and joint nonreuse with H.
It does not introduce a new kind of defect or a second count of that strip.

Assignments of these actual claims to J are injective, jointly with all
previous short-H and [long-H](LONG_HORIZONTAL_CORNER_CHANNEL.md)
claim-to-lower-pair assignments. Enlarge the
existing physical lower-pair union by these tiles, taking each tile once.
Do not add a receipt if that physical pair was already in the union.

## 2. The first consumer and both independent dimensions

The original X support q=(b+1,3) has neighbors north in X, west and south
in B, and is an endpoint of neither. Its actual consumer has only an east
own neighbor. As in the maximal-band proof, the two east rotor tips collide
with X at (b,4) or B at (b+2,4). A U consumer is an east terminal.

Write its incident leg g, width s and other leg t. Its stem on row b+1
hits B at (b+1,11) if g>=8, so evenness gives g<=6. A downward bar
hits B at (b+2,g+3). An upward bar with g=4 hits X at (b,7), which
exists because X's far leg has length four. Hence g=6 and the upward
frame is Q(r,c)=(b+2-c,9-r).

If s>=b, Q's intrinsic bar point (0,b) maps to (2,9). This is a C
endpoint: C(h-7,1)=(2,9), with h>=8 and h-7 between1 and h. Thus s<b,
and evenness gives s<=b-2. This step uses C and works also at a=4;
one must not assume X's near stem always reaches column9.

Put r0=b+2-s, so 4<=r0<=b-4. If t>=6, Q's far leg contains intrinsic
(5,s), mapping to (r0,4). This belongs to X's original bar: intrinsic
(1,r0-2), with 2<=r0-2<=b-6<b-2. Thus t=4.

The consumer differs from B,X,C because q is absent from their endpoint
sets. The C exclusion follows from q's column3 and physical row b+1>=9:
C's near stem has row2, its far stem row V+1, and its bar columns h+1,h+2
are at least9. If V=b, C's far stem could have row b+1, but q still
cannot be its endpoint in an endpoint-disjoint occurrence: C at (b+1,3)
would also contain (b+1,2), already B-owned. Equivalently its row-b+1
far interval is [h+2-f,h+2], so inclusion of3 implies inclusion of2
only when h+2-f<=2; inclusion of3 with lower end3 is excluded by parity,
since that lower end is even. Hence q is not C-owned. This owner check
is necessary before using the C collision.

The frame reflection constants b+2 (even) and9 (odd), after axis exchange,
give the negative charge -1 phase. Q's original terminal supports at q
are (b+1,2),(b+2,3), both B-owned. Their consumption does not turn Q into
a positive source.

## 3. Three columns protect the pair at every b and s

The ENTIRE lattice boundary of

    D=[r0,b+1] x [4,8]

consists of original X/Q endpoints. The top side is X at column4 and Q
at columns5..8; the bottom side is Q at every column4..8. X's lower bar
supplies column4 on rows r0..b, and Q supplies its last point at b+1.
Q's bar supplies column8 on all rows r0..b+1.

Inside D, X has only its far-leg endpoints on row b, at columns5..7.
Its near leg has row3<r0, and its bar has columns3,4. Q has no strict
interior endpoint. The following argument excludes every OTHER original
owner, including B and C, without a separate interval argument for each.

Indeed an interior endpoint of a foreign source would confine its entire
connected endpoint graph to the strict interior: any unit path leaving
would meet the X/Q-owned boundary. The strict interior has only three
columns, namely5,6,7. Every U has extent at least five in every axis;
every rotor has extent four. Neither fits, in any orientation or at any
dimensions. Thus foreign interior endpoints are impossible. X's own
interior far leg does not weaken this exclusion, since a foreign consumer
differs from BOTH boundary owners.

The missing corners (b-2,5),(b-1,5) of J are strictly inside D because
r0<=b-4. They are not X endpoints, whose interior endpoints have row b;
nor are they Q endpoints. So they can never become endpoints. Each has
an X endpoint immediately to its west. If it were selected, that endpoint
would have to be its unique original anchor, hence X would have to produce
it as an original support. Its X coordinates are (2,b-4) or (2,b-3),
neither an original support: row2 supports lie only at columns0 and b-1.
Thus neither point is selected either. The original X profile of J is
exactly a lower-bar irregular pair, with no original intratile support.

For explicit comparison with the old lower-strip theorem, use the chart
Phi(r,c)=(b+3-c,r), and exchange X's legs. In that chart the SAME sources are

    D0=U(b-2,4,a), D0(r,c)=(r+3,c+2),
    E=U(s,6,4), E(r,c)=(9-r,c+1).

These are exactly its two source frames. Its parameter m=min(b,s+1)=s+1
and its strip has s/2-2 distinct pairs. The first one is J after Phi.
All those strip pairs can be included in the physical lower-pair set once.
If that set already includes all old lower-strip enclosures, J was already
present: the new result is an injective demand assignment to that capacity.

## 4. Injection and comparison with the old H targets

    Phi(C_old(r,c))=(b+1-h_old+r,c+1).

New pair tiles are disjoint from upper-strip pairs: for the same owner
