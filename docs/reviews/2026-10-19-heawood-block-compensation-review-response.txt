Verdict: REVISEThe proposed inequality
$$5|S|+2b\le 3|V(G)|$$
and the consequent value  $SF_2(L_k)=36k$  appear mathematically plausible, and I found no counterexample to the stated theorem in the supplied local checks. The ledger signs and the intended no-reuse mechanism are essentially correct. However, the attached proof is not yet at the standard for an unconditional ACCEPT: the closed-Heawood classification contains two genuine omitted arguments, and the restoration step depends on a certificate whose precise scope must be stated. These are locally repairable gaps, not evidence that the theorem is false.1. The global signs and capacity identity are correctWith
$$a(v)=|N(v)\cap S|,$$
the total demand of selected high vertices is
$$\sum_{v\in S}a(v)-|S|+i,$$
where  $i$  is the number of selected isolates. The total omitted capacity is
$$\sum_{x\notin S}(3-a(x)).$$
Using  $\sum_{v\in V(G)}a(v)=3|S|$ , their difference is
$$3|V(G)|-5|S|-i.$$
Thus the identity
$$3n-5|S|=i+W
\tag{1}$$
has the correct sign.For a block  $B$ , if  $s_B=|S\cap B|$ ,  $q_B$  is the number of selected ports,  $p_B$  the number of selected adjacent core vertices, and  $i_B$  the number of selected isolates in the block, then
$$\sum_{v\in B}a(v)=3s_B-q_B+p_B.$$
The  $-q_B$  comes from selected ports whose attachment edge is not counted inside the block, while  $+p_B$  comes from selected external core neighbors adjacent to omitted ports. Consequently,
$$W_B+F_B
=
42-5s_B-i_B+q_B-p_B,
\tag{2}$$
where  $F_B=E_B-I_B$  is export demand minus import demand. This sign is also correct.Writing  $\delta_B=8-s_B$ , equation (2) becomes
$$i_B+W_B
=
2+5\delta_B+q_B-p_B-F_B.$$
Therefore the desired local estimate
$$F_B+p_B-q_B\le 5\delta_B+t_B
\tag{3}$$
does imply
$$i_B+W_B+t_B\ge 2.
\tag{4}$$
So the algebraic route from the local inequality to the global bound is sound.2. The global no-reuse summation is correct, with one clarificationThe intended summation
$$2b
\le
\sum_B(i_B+W_B+t_B)
\le i+W$$
is valid under the stated hypotheses, provided the bookkeeping is interpreted exactly as written: $W_B$  is residual capacity after all witness demands have been assigned, not raw block capacity.The witness map for high vertices is injective, so one omitted resource cannot be assigned to two different high owners.A capacity-two resource can pay a degree-three demand of two, or a degree-two demand of one with the remaining unit entering  $W_B$ ; that remaining unit is not silently reused.A selected core isolate counted by  $t_B$  is counted once globally because each core vertex is incident with at most one block.A selected isolate may appear in several private clauses inside one block, as happens in the six-cycle-plus-two-isolates pattern, but it is counted once as a vertex in  $i_B$ , not once per clause.The proof should explicitly say that selected isolates are support vertices, not capacity resources. Otherwise “used twice” can sound ambiguous.3. The  $s_B\le 7$  cases are basically soundThe interface bounds
$$E_{\text{interface}}+p-q
\le
\begin{cases}
0,&\text{core omitted},\\
2,&\text{core selected and port selected},\\
3,&\text{core selected and port omitted}
\end{cases}$$
are consistent with the witness types:a selected port can export only if it is a selected leaf of the external core high and an internal omitted resource has that port as its unique selected neighbor;an omitted port can export through the port itself, with capacity at most two;the value  $3$  requires an omitted port with no selected internal neighbor.Thus the total is at most  $6$ . For  $s_B\le 6$ ,  $6\le5\delta_B$ . For  $s_B=7$ , exceeding  $5$  would require the value  $3$  at both ports, which is excluded by the two-bare-port lemma.At size eight, the  $q_B=2$  argument also works once the export characterization is made explicit: a two-neighbor omitted resource cannot witness deletion of a core high through a selected port, because deleting the core high leaves the selected port itself still present, so the resource still has two selected neighbors. Hence a selected-port export requires precisely the isolated-port/one-neighbor-resource configuration excluded by H2.4. The exactly-one-selected-port branch is logically the right branch, but needs precise wordingLet  $\pi$  be the omitted port and  $\lambda$  the selected port.The important case is
$$q_B=1,\qquad p_B=2,$$
with both core neighbors  $a,b$  selected and active.The proof’s conclusion is correct:Any export can occur only through  $\pi$ .H1 rules out the capacity-two export through a port with no selected internal neighbor.Therefore an export through  $\pi$  has demand at most one.H3 forces an import of two through  $\lambda$ , because  $b$  is selected.Thus, if an export occurs,  $F_B\le -1$ , exactly as required.The delicate subcase is “no export and  $I_B=0$ .” The proposed restoration argument can be made valid, but it should be rewritten as follows.Detach both external edges  $\pi a$  and  $\lambda b$ .Restore the deleted Heawood edge  $\pi\lambda$ .Work in the resulting closed Heawood graph.Formation is preserved: $\pi$  loses selected  $a$  but gains selected  $\lambda$ ; H1 guarantees another selected internal neighbor of  $\pi$ , so  $\pi$  remains two-covered.An omitted internal vertex can lose coverage only if  $\lambda$  ceases to be active and was its unique selected neighbor. That requires  $\lambda$  to have no selected internal neighbor and a one-neighbor resource, which H2 excludes.For the private-clause argument, the sentence“ $a$  is active through a selected core neighbor, since  $\pi$  is omitted”is causally incorrect. The fact that  $\pi$  is omitted does not imply that  $a$  is active. In this branch, however,  $a$  is active by the branch assumption. Since  $\pi$  is its only block neighbor, a selected core neighbor  $c\in N_K(a)$  appears in the clause  $B_{(\pi,a)}$ , and  $c\neq v$  for any selected non-port  $v$ . Therefore  $B_{(\pi,a)}$  cannot be private to  $v$ . This is the correct argument.If  $\lambda$  has no selected internal neighbor, it becomes isolated after detachment and is self-private. Otherwise,  $I_B=0$  gives an internal failure witness for  $\lambda$ , which remains a failure witness after removing the external edges and adding the omitted-omitted edge after  $\lambda$  is deleted.Now apply the closed-Heawood classification:If  $\lambda$  lies on the selected six-cycle, then in the original broken block its two internal neighbors are selected highs, it has no omitted internal neighbor, and it has no selected leaf. Therefore it has no internal  $B$ -witness. Since  $b$  is selected, its original selected degree is three, so its demand is two;  $I_B=0$  is impossible.If  $\lambda$  is one of the two isolates, its two internal omitted neighbors are the unique witnesses for the adjacent cycle highs. The selected external neighbor  $b$  makes  $\lambda$  active, so those omitted vertices remain formed after deleting the adjacent cycle high. The cycle highs have no alternate leaf witnesses. This contradicts global minimality.Therefore the no-export,  $I_B=0$  branch is impossible, and  $I_B\ge1$  follows.5. The closed-Heawood classification has a genuine proof gapThe conclusion is consistent with the supplied finite validation: every minimal eight-selection is a six-cycle plus two isolates, and every omitted vertex has one cycle-high neighbor and one isolate neighbor. But two logical steps are omitted in the written proof.5.1 “Every degree-three high needs a leaf” must be provedThe text says:“Every degree-three high needs such a leaf.”This does not follow merely from the preceding discussion unless the failure types are invoked explicitly.The missing argument is:A selected high of degree three has all three graph neighbors selected.Therefore it cannot have an adjacent omitted witness of type 1 or type 2.Its deletion witness must be type 3.Type 3 requires a selected leaf whose unique selected support is that high.Thus every degree-three high has at least one selected leaf.Conversely:A selected degree-two high cannot have a selected leaf, because the corresponding one-neighbor resource has capacity two while the high has demand one, leaving unused capacity and contradicting  $W=0$ .A degree-three high cannot have two leaves, because the two leaves require distinct capacity-two resources, while the high has only demand two; one resource would leave unused capacity.Hence each degree-three high has exactly one leaf, and every degree-two high has none. The induced graph on selected highs is therefore 2-regular.This is a substantive missing inference because the six-cycle classification depends on it.5.2 The appeal to  $\beta_2(H)=7$  does not by itself exclude “no highs”The text says that the high graph is nonempty because otherwise the eight-selection would “dissociate,” contradicting  $\beta_2(H)=7$ . If  $\beta_2(H)=7$  means only that the minimum forming-set size is seven, that does not by itself rule out a minimal forming eight-set with no high vertices.A self-contained exclusion is short. Suppose there are no highs. Since  $i=2$ , the six other selected vertices have selected degree one, so they form three disjoint selected edges. Let  $p$  and  $q$  be the numbers of selected points and selected lines. Then  $p+q=8$  and  $p,q\ge3$ , so  $p\in\{3,4,5\}$ .With no highs, there is no high demand. Since  $W=0$ , every omitted vertex has capacity zero, hence all three of its neighbors are selected. In particular, every omitted line contains three selected points. The number of omitted lines is
$$7-q = p-1.$$
Each omitted line contributes three distinct pairs of selected points, and in the Fano plane any pair of points lies on a unique line. Therefore
$$3(p-1)\le \binom p2.$$
For  $p=3,4,5$ , this reads respectively
$$6\le3,\qquad 9\le6,\qquad 12\le10,$$
all impossible. Thus the high set is nonempty.Once these two missing steps are inserted, the remainder of the classification follows:the high graph is 2-regular;Heawood girth six and only six selected non-isolates force one six-cycle;there are no selected leaves or matching components;each high has demand one; $W=0$  forces six distinct capacity-one resources, each adjacent to one cycle high and one isolate;there are exactly six omitted vertices, so every omitted vertex has that form.6. The two prior Fano lemmas are usable after local correctionsI did not find a counterexample to the stated bare-port or saturated-transfer conclusions. Their roles in H1–H4 are appropriate. A few passages should nevertheless be corrected.In the bare-port proof, the  $p=3$ ,  $M=L$  case says that intersections on  $L$  have selected degree three. In  $B=H-PL$ , with  $L$  omitted, those points have selected degree two. The argument still works because their other selected line neighbor remains active, but the sentence must be changed.In the selected-isolated-port analysis, the surviving  $ab\cap L=R$  case should be written explicitly as the directional template. The current “only intersection  $R$  remains” is understandable but too compressed.The H2-to- $E_B=0$  implication should state why a two-neighbor resource cannot export demand through a selected port. H2 excludes the only possible one-neighbor-resource configuration; the capacity argument should be written rather than left implicit.In the  $q_B=0$  eight-selection branch, the proof must state the precise scope of the cited “attachment certificate theorem.” To detach the external edges and invoke the closed-Heawood classification, one needs:internal formation for all omitted block vertices after detachment; andan internal private clause for every selected non-port.The latter is not automatic. A boundary clause on an interface edge could otherwise be the only private clause for a selected non-port and could disappear when the external edge is removed. If the cited attachment certificate already proves this for arbitrary ambient core restrictions, its exact statement should be inserted. If not, this is an additional proof obligation.7. Validation evidence does not close these gapsThe 28 closed minimal eight-sets, the boundary-state enumeration, and the conditional Lean ledger checks are useful consistency checks. They do not replace the missing general implications:enumeration cannot prove the degree-three-high leaf argument for arbitrary ambient use;enumeration cannot justify the detachment/restoration step;the conditional ledger formalization does not by itself prove the graph-to-ledger extraction or the Fano restoration classification.Final assessmentThe theorem itself appears correct under the stated hypotheses, and the central compensation ledger has the right signs and locality. The exactly-one-selected-port, two-active-core-neighbors, no-imports branch can also be closed by the intended restoration argument.The submitted proof should nevertheless be marked REVISE, because the closed-Heawood classification currently omits the degree-three-high implication and gives an insufficient reason for the existence of a high. The  $q=0$  detachment step also needs the exact attachment-certificate scope stated. After those repairs, together with the small Fano wording corrections above, I would regard the argument as a candidate for ACCEPT.
