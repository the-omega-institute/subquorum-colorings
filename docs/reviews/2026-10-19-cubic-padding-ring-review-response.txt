The deficiency identity and its equality consequencesLet  $D$  be a dissociation set in a graph  $F$  of maximum degree at most three, and put  $T=V(F)\setminus D$ . Since every nonisolated vertex of  $F[D]$  belongs to an isolated edge,
$$2e(F[D])=|D|-i_D.$$
Consequently,
$$e(D,T)
=3|D|-R_D-2e(F[D])
=2|D|-R_D+i_D.$$
Counting from  $T$  gives
$$e(D,T)
=3(N-|D|)-(R-R_D)-2e_T.$$
Equating these expressions yields exactly
$$\boxed{3N-5|D|-R+2R_D=i_D+2e_T.}$$
For  $Q_k$ , the four distinct ports each have degree deficit one. Thus  $R=4$ ,  $R_D=p$ , and
$$5|D|\le60k-4+2p\le60k+4.$$
Integrality gives  $|D|\le12k$ .Now suppose  $x_0,x_1\notin D$  and  $|D|=12k$ . There are only two remaining ports, so  $p\le2$ , while the identity gives
$$2p-4=i_D+2e_T\ge0.$$
Therefore, simultaneously,
$$p=2,\qquad i_D=0,\qquad e_T=0.$$
This proves all three required conclusions: $x_2,x_3\in D$ ;every vertex of  $D$  has exactly one neighbor in  $D$  inside  $Q_k$ ; $T$  is independent inside  $Q_k$ .The same deductions hold with the two cut pairs exchanged. The equality argument uses the actual four vertex deficits exactly once.2. 5. Two-connectivity holds for every ring lengthThe connectivity argument is a valid instance of the following general lemma.Ring lemma. Let  $G_1,\ldots,G_r$  be disjoint two-connected graphs, with distinguished edges  $a_ib_i$ . Delete these edges and add  $a_ib_{i+1}$  cyclically. The resulting graph is two-connected.For  $r=1$ , the operation restores the deleted edge.For  $r\ge2$ , each  $F_i=G_i-a_ib_i$  is connected. Delete a vertex  $v\in G_j$ .If  $v$  is neither port,  $G_j-v$  is connected. Removing  $a_jb_j$  leaves at most two components, with the two ports in different components if a split occurs. The other  $r-1$  connected blocks and the ring interfaces supply an external path from  $a_j$  to  $b_j$ , reconnecting those pieces.If  $v=a_j$ , the surviving part of its block is exactly  $G_j-a_j$ , which is connected. The remaining ring interfaces form a chain through the other blocks attached to  $b_j$ .Deleting  $b_j$  gives the symmetric case.For  $r=2$ , the external path in the first case runs through the single other block, and the port-deletion cases retain one connecting interface. Thus the proof explicitly includes  $r=2$ .The first Heawood/core edge swap is this lemma with two graphs. The final operation applies it to the resulting  $G_i$ .The needed two-connectivity of  $C_k$  also follows directly from its definition. The two cycles and the paths  $h_i-u_i-x_i$  remain connected after deleting any one vertex. Each added  $Y$ -vertex has three distinct neighbors in that graph; adjoining a vertex with at least two distinct neighbors preserves two-connectivity. This covers  $k=1$  as well.6.
