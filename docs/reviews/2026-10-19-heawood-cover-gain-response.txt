The claim is true. There is a structural proof using an exact matrix model of the switching classes, the rational canonical forms of traceless  $3\times3$  binary matrices, and six explicit dissociation sets.The proof also gives the proposed rigidity statement:
$$\boxed{\beta_2(H)=14\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the signing is switch-trivial}.}$$
1. Switching classes are traceless binary matricesPut  $V=\mathbb F_2^3$ . Represent a Fano point by a nonzero column  $p\in V$ , and a Fano line by a nonzero row  $\ell\in V^*$ . Incidence means
$$\ell p=0.$$
This is precisely the given Fano plane: the three nonzero points in  $\ker\ell$  form a triple  $\{a,b,a+b\}$ .For an edge signing  $\sigma$ , define
$$\Phi(\sigma)=
\sum_{\ell p=0}\sigma(p,\ell)\,p\ell
\in\operatorname{End}(V).$$
Here  $p\ell$  is the rank-one operator  $v\mapsto\ell(v)p$ . Since
$$\operatorname{tr}(p\ell)=\ell p=0,$$
the image lies in the eight-dimensional space
$$\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb F_2)
=\{M:\operatorname{tr}M=0\}.$$
Lemma 1. The map  $\Phi$  induces an isomorphism
$$\mathbb F_2^{E}/\{\text{vertex-switching signatures}\}
\;\cong\;\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb F_2).$$
Proof. Switching at a point  $p$  changes  $\Phi$  by
$$p\left(\sum_{\substack{\ell\ne0\\\ell p=0}}\ell\right)=0.$$
The sum vanishes because the three nonzero vectors in a two-dimensional binary vector space sum to zero. Switching at a line similarly changes  $\Phi$  by
$$\left(\sum_{\substack{p\ne0\\\ell p=0}}p\right)\ell=0.$$
Thus  $\Phi$  annihilates the switching space.It is also surjective. Writing  $E_{ij}$  for the matrix units, the single flag  $(e_i,e_j^*)$ , for  $i\ne j$ , maps to  $E_{ij}$ . Moreover,
$$(e_i+e_j)(e_i^*+e_j^*)+E_{ij}+E_{ji}
=E_{ii}+E_{jj}.$$
These give all six off-diagonal directions and the two independent traceless diagonal directions.Therefore
$$\dim\ker\Phi=21-8=13.$$
The switching space has dimension  $14-1=13$ : the kernel of the vertex-to-edge coboundary map consists of the constant vertex assignments, since the Heawood graph is connected. Consequently,
$$\ker\Phi=\{\text{switch-trivial signings}\}.
\qquad\square$$
A coordinate change  $g\in GL_3(\mathbb F_2)$  acts on flags by
$$(p,\ell)\longmapsto(gp,\ell g^{-1}),$$
and transports the invariant according to
$$\Phi(g\cdot\sigma)=g\Phi(\sigma)g^{-1}.$$
It follows that signings whose matrices are conjugate give isomorphic covering graphs, after a Fano coordinate change and vertex sheet switching.In particular, connected two-sheeted covers correspond to nonzero matrices under  $\Phi$ .2. A complete algebraic reduction to six representativesA traceless  $3\times3$  binary matrix has characteristic polynomial
$$\chi_M(t)=t^3+at+b,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb F_2.$$
The following argument proves the completeness of the six cases. $\chi_M(t)=t^3$ . Cayley–Hamilton gives  $M^3=0$ . If  $M\ne0$  and  $M^2=0$ , then  $\operatorname{im}M\subseteq\ker M$  forces rank one, giving the nilpotent type  $2+1$ . If  $M^2\ne0$ , a vector  $v$  with  $M^2v\ne0$  gives the basis  $(M^2v,Mv,v)$ , hence the nilpotent type  $3$ . $\chi_M(t)=t^3+t=t(t+1)^2$ . Primary decomposition gives a one-dimensional zero block and a two-dimensional eigenvalue-one block. The latter is either  $I_2$  or  $J_2(1)$ . $\chi_M(t)=t^3+1=(t+1)(t^2+t+1)$ . The coprime primary factors give one linear block and the unique irreducible quadratic block, hence one similarity type. $\chi_M(t)=t^3+t+1$ . This polynomial has no root in  $\mathbb F_2$ , so it is irreducible. $$\boxed{
\beta_2(H)=14
\iff
\Phi(\sigma)=0
\iff
\sigma\text{ is switch-trivial}.

For completeness, a dissociation set  $D$  in a cubic graph on  $28$  vertices satisfies
$$2|D|\le e(D,V\setminus D)\le3(28-|D|),$$
because every selected vertex has at least two neighbors outside  $D$ . Therefore
$$\beta_2(H)\le\left\lfloor\frac{84}{5}\right\rfloor=16.$$
The connected two-sheeted covers consequently satisfy
$$\beta_2(H)\in\{15,16\}.$$
