$$\boxed{SF_2(L_k)=36k\qquad\text{for every }k\ge1.}$$
There is a general proof. The useful inequality is a strengthened cubic order count: every inclusion-minimal forming set  $S$  in  $L_k$  satisfies
$$3|V(L_k)|-5|S|\ge 6k. \tag{1}$$
Since  $|V(L_k)|=62k$ , this gives  $|S|\le36k$ .The proof assigns a precise cost to the support supplied by each Heawood block. A block contributes at least two units of selected isolates or unused private-witness capacity. In one boundary case, it uses an adjacent selected core isolate; that isolate belongs to exactly one block, so this charge cannot be reused.I give the accounting first, followed by the geometric proofs of the required Heawood facts. The proof uses your established robust eight-vertex block cap, one-cut saturation, and isolate compensation. It does not require the value of  $\beta_2(L_k)$ , enumeration, or a solver premise.1. Exact private witnesses and their capacitiesFix a minimal forming set  $S$  in a cubic bipartite graph. Write  $d_S(v)=|N(v)\cap S|$ . Call a selected vertex high if  $d_S(v)\ge2$ .Use the following notation for outside vertices:A  $P$ -vertex has exactly one selected neighbor.A  $Q$ -vertex has exactly two selected neighbors.For a selected high vertex  $v$ , minimality supplies one of three witnesses:A  $P$ -vertex whose selected neighbor is  $v$ .A  $Q$ -vertex with selected neighbors  $v,w$ , where  $w$  is a selected isolate.A selected leaf  $u$  of  $v$ , together with a  $P$ -vertex whose selected neighbor is  $u$ .These possibilities are exhaustive. After deleting  $v$ , an outside vertex becomes unformed precisely when it loses its last selected neighbor, or its sole remaining selected neighbor becomes isolated. Bipartiteness ensures that, in the direct  $Q$  case, the other selected neighbor was already isolated.Give each selected high  $v$  demand
$$d_S(v)-1,$$
so a selected vertex of degree two has demand one, and one of degree three has demand two.Give an outside vertex  $w$  capacity
$$c(w)=3-d_S(w).$$
Thus  $P$ -vertices have capacity two and  $Q$ -vertices have capacity one. Outside vertices of selected degree three have capacity zero.Each witness has enough capacity for its high:A degree-three high has no outside neighbor, so its witness must be a  $P$ -vertex reached through a selected leaf.A degree-two high needs only one unit.Moreover, an outside resource can witness at most one high. A  $P$ -vertex has one selected neighbor; if that neighbor is a leaf, it has only one selected parent. A usable  $Q$ -vertex has only one nonisolated selected neighbor.Choose one witness for every high, pay its demand from that witness, and let  $W$  be the total unused capacity. Then  $W\ge0$ , with no resource reused.If  $i$  is the number of selected isolates, the exact count is
$$\boxed{3n-5|S|=i+W.} \tag{2}$$
Indeed, with
$$C=\sum_{w\notin S}(3-d_S(w)),\qquad
D=\sum_{\substack{v\in S\\d_S(v)\ge2}}(d_S(v)-1),$$
we have  $W=C-D$ , and
$$D=\sum_{v\in S}d_S(v)-|S|+i.$$
Since  $\sum_{v\in V(G)}d_S(v)=3|S|$ , equation (2) follows.We will prove that the Heawood blocks force  $i+W\ge6k$ .2. The exact count across one Heawood blockLet  $B$  be one attached broken Heawood block. Put
$$s_B=|S\cap B|,\qquad \delta_B=8-s_B\ge0.$$
The nonnegativity of  $\delta_B$  is your established robust block cap.Also write: $q_B$ : number of selected Heawood ports; $p_B$ : number of selected adjacent core  $X$ -vertices; $i_B$ : selected isolates lying in  $B$ , measured in the full graph; $W_B$ : unused capacity at outside vertices belonging to  $B$ ; $E_B$ : demand of core highs paid by resources in  $B$ ; $I_B$ : demand of block highs paid by resources in the core; $F_B=E_B-I_B$ .A witness crossing this interface connects a block resource to a core high, or a core resource to a block high. Its length is at most two, so it cannot pass through another Heawood block.The local capacity-minus-demand identity is
$$\boxed{
W_B+F_B=42-5s_B-i_B+q_B-p_B.
} \tag{3}$$
The boundary sign is worth checking explicitly. Summing selected-neighbor counts over the fourteen vertices of  $B$  gives
$$\sum_{v\in B}d_S(v)=3s_B-q_B+p_B.$$
A selected port contributes one adjacency leaving  $B$ , while a selected external  $X$ -vertex contributes one adjacency entering  $B$ . Substituting this into the calculation behind (2) proves (3).Let  $t_B$  count the selected isolated core  $X$ -vertices adjacent to  $B$ . The local estimate we need is
$$\boxed{
F_B+p_B-q_B\le 5\delta_B+t_B.
} \tag{4}$$
Equations (3) and (4) give
$$\boxed{i_B+W_B+t_B\ge2.} \tag{5}$$
