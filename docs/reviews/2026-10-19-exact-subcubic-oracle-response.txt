VerdictThe local cap follows rigorously under the stated assumptions. I find no counterexample or substantive gap in the robust deletion criterion, its upward closure, the seven-set case analysis, or its application to arbitrary outside graphs. Consequently, the assumed attaining construction and the local cap give
$$SF_2(J_r)=7r.$$
The two points most deserving explicit clarification are:When an omitted vertex retains two selected neighbors after deletion, it had at least three before deletion, so the first alternative of condition 3 applies.When replacing the deleted leaf in a former external star, the replacement leaf cannot equal the vertex being covered: the replacement belongs to the original selected set, while the covered vertex does not.Both facts already follow from the proof’s hypotheses. They require clarification, not a change to the argument.The review below checks the rules directly from the displayed neighborhoods, without relying on finite enumeration or reported Lean checks.1. The robust deletion criterion has the right strengthFix  $C\subseteq V(K)$  and a proposed deletion  $v\in C$ . Write
$$W=V(K)\setminus C,\qquad H=C\setminus\{v\}.$$
Call a vertex active in  $H$  if it has a neighbor in  $H$ .Condition 2 says exactly that every selected neighbor of  $v$  remains active after deletion:
$$u\in N_K(v)\cap C
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_K(u)\cap H\ne\varnothing.$$
This condition serves two separate purposes. It supplies a star covering the newly outside vertex  $v$ , and it preserves stars in which  $v$  had been a leaf.The exact content of condition 3For an omitted neighbor  $x\in W\cap N_K(v)$ , one has
$$|N_K(x)\cap C|=|N_K(x)\cap H|+1.$$
In the presence of condition 2, condition 3 is therefore equivalent to the following statement after deletion:Either  $x$  has at least two neighbors in  $H$ , or it has exactly one neighbor  $u$  in  $H$ , and  $u$  is active in  $H$ .The only potentially delicate implication is the second alternative in the original condition 3. Suppose
$$N_K(x)\cap C=\{v,u\},$$
and  $u$  has a neighbor  $w\in C$ .If  $w\ne v$ , then  $w\in H$ , as needed. If  $w=v$ , then  $u$  is itself a selected neighbor of  $v$ , and condition 2 supplies a neighbor
$$w'\in C\setminus\{v\}=H$$
of  $u$ . Thus  $u$  remains active in either event.This justifies the manuscript’s explanation of why the “other” selected neighbor remains active. Merely counting two selected neighbors before deletion would indeed be insufficient.For the case checks below, I will use this equivalent formulation after deletion. It keeps the distinction between the two alternatives visible.2. Lemma 1: upward closure is correctThe quantification should be understood as
$$C\subseteq C'\subseteq V(K).$$
Condition 1 plainly persists. For condition 2, there are two kinds of selected neighbors of  $v$  in  $C'$ .An old selected neighbor  $u\in C$ . Its replacement neighbor in  $C\setminus\{v\}$  remains in  $C'\setminus\{v\}$ .A newly selected neighbor  $u\in C'\setminus C$ . This vertex was an omitted neighbor of  $v$  for the original set  $C$ . Condition 3 for  $C$  says that  $u$  had at least two neighbors in  $C$ . One is  $v$ , so another belongs to
$$C\setminus\{v\}\subseteq C'\setminus\{v\}.$$
Thus every newly selected neighbor also satisfies condition 2.For condition 3, let  $x\notin C'$  be adjacent to  $v$ . Then  $x\notin C$ , so the original condition applies.If  $x$  originally had at least three selected neighbors, it still does.If it originally had exactly two, then either it acquires another selected neighbor, satisfying the first alternative, or it still has its original two. In the latter case, the neighbor other than  $v$  retains its original selected neighbor in  $C'$ .There is no circularity here: condition 2 for  $C'$ , including its newly selected neighbors, has already been established.Consequently, proving Lemma 2 for seven-element sets suffices. Given any larger  $C$ , choose a seven-element subset  $C_0\subseteq C$ , find a robust deletion vertex for  $C_0$ , and apply upward closure.3. Lemma 3: the deletion works in every permitted outside graphLet
$$C=S\cap V(K),\qquad S'=S\setminus\{v\},$$
where  $v$  is supplied by Lemma 2.The induced-copy assumption and the attachment restriction together give
$$N_G(v)=N_K(v),$$
because  $v\notin A$ . This equality is the precise fact needed when transferring the internal deletion conditions to  $G$ .The vertices outside  $S'$  are the newly outside vertex  $v$  and the vertices that were already outside  $S$ . The proof treats all of them.The newly outside vertex  $v$ Condition 1 provides
$$u\in N_K(v)\cap C.$$
Condition 2 then provides
$$w\in N_K(u)\cap(C\setminus\{v\}).$$
Thus  $u,w\in S'$ , with edges  $vu,uw$ . These are three distinct vertices: $u\ne v$ , because they are adjacent in a simple graph; $w\ne v$ , by construction; $w\ne u$ , again because the graph is simple.Therefore  $v-u-w$  is a valid star through  $v$ .An old outside vertex  $x$  adjacent to  $v$ Since  $v$  has no external neighbors,  $x\in V(K)$ . Since  $x\notin S$ , it belongs to  $W$ .Condition 3, with the survival justification from Section 1 above, gives either:two selected neighbors remaining in  $S'$ , so  $x$  is the center of a star; ora selected neighbor  $u\in S'$  that has a surviving selected neighbor  $w\in S'$ , so  $x-u-w$  is a star.All of these witnesses lie inside the core, so additional external edges or external selected vertices are immaterial.An old outside vertex  $x$  not adjacent to  $v$ Take a former witnessing copy of  $K_{1,2}$  through  $x$  in  $G[S\cup\{x\}]$ .If it does not use  $v$ , it survives.If it uses  $v$ , then  $x$  and  $v$  must be its two leaves. Indeed, if either were the center, the edge  $xv$  would be required, contrary to this case’s assumption. Write the former star as
$$x-u-v,$$
with selected center  $u\in S$ .Because  $u$  is adjacent to  $v$ , the identity  $N_G(v)=N_K(v)$  implies
$$u\in N_K(v)\cap C.$$
Condition 2 supplies
$$w\in N_K(u)\cap(C\setminus\{v\}).$$
Hence  $u,w\in S'$ , and  $x-u-w$  is the proposed replacement star.The distinctness of the replacement leaf is fully justified:
$$w\ne v\quad\text{by construction},\qquad
w\ne u\quad\text{by simplicity},\qquad
w\ne x\quad\text{because }w\in S\text{ and }x\notin S.$$
This last fact closes the possible concern about replacing a deleted leaf in an external star. The center  $u$  may be  $3$  or  $11$ , and  $x$  may be anywhere outside the core. Condition 2 still supplies an internal selected replacement leaf.Any additional edge  $xw$  would cause no difficulty because the required copy of  $K_{1,2}$  is non-induced.Why this proves the unrestricted local capEvery vertex outside  $S'$  is covered, so  $S'$  is 2-star-forming. It is a proper subset of  $S$ , contradicting inclusion-minimality.The proof uses no restriction on the exterior beyond the permitted attachment locations. In particular, it does not require  $C$  itself to be star-forming inside  $K$ : the original witnessing stars come from the global star-forming set  $S$ , and the robust conditions guarantee their preservation.Therefore
$$|S\cap V(K)|\le6$$
holds for every finite simple outside graph allowed by Lemma 3.5. The exact cyclic value and necessary equality structure followLet  $K_1,\dots,K_r$  be the disjoint cores and let  $P$  be the set of  $r$  shared ports. For any inclusion-minimal 2-star-forming set  $S$ , put
$$c_i=|S\cap V(K_i)|,\qquad p=|S\cap P|.$$
Applying Lemma 3 separately to every core gives
$$c_i\le6,\qquad p\le r.$$
Because the cores and the port set partition the vertices,
$$|S|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_i+p\le6r+r=7r.$$
The attaining minimal set specified in the question has six selected vertices in each core and all  $r$  ports selected. Its assumed minimality therefore gives the matching lower bound, proving
$$\boxed{SF_2(J_r)=7r.}$$
For equality, the cleanest argument is the deficit identity
$$7r-|S|
=
\sum_{i=1}^{r}(6-c_i)+(r-p).$$
Every summand is nonnegative. If  $|S|=7r$ , every summand must vanish:
$$c_i=6\quad\text{for every }i,\qquad p=r.$$
This proves exactly the stated necessary equality condition. It does not assert that every choice of six vertices per core, together with all ports, is star-forming or minimal.The identities involving  $\Gamma,\alpha,\beta_2$  remain dependent on their earlier proofs; they are not additional consequences of the new local lemma.
