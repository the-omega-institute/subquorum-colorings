Black  $Q$ -vertices are sources and white  $Q$ -vertices are sinks. Consequently the components are terminal paths and directed  $P$ -only cycles.When an edge belongs to  $M\cap N$ , retaining its two tagged copies is essential. They form a directed cycle on two physical vertices. Deleting the  $N$ -copy at a candidate cut leaves the actual  $M$ -copy intact.After deleting the  $2e$  candidate  $N$ -copies, the starts are precisely:The  $n$  original black  $Q$ -vertices.The  $2e$  white endpoints of the deleted copies.These starts are distinct. The two classes have different colours, and the cut endpoints are distinct because the candidate squares have disjoint vertex supports. A start may also be an endpoint of its fragment, producing a singleton.The predecessor argument proves the partition, rather than merely counting its expected pieces. Exact coverage and the terminal-normalized cutCoverage is exactly  $B_T-F$ For any routing with  $a'$  residual TT paths, exactly  $B_T-a'$  terminal paths run from black  $T$  to white  $H$ . Their fragment arcs form a feasible unit-capacity flow. Hence
$$B_T-a'\le F,
\qquad
a'\ge B_T-F.$$
Conversely, take an integral maximum flow. At each switch, the incoming and outgoing marked-port counts agree. A self-loop contributes once to each count. For two ports on each side, equal counts guarantee that either the straight or crossed bijection preserves the marked/unmarked labels.After decoding those local choices, a marked path starting at a source cannot enter a closed routed cycle. A cycle already supplies the unique predecessor of each of its fragments; an entering path would violate that uniqueness. Thus every used source arc reaches a white  $H$ -terminal.This gives at least  $F$  successful black- $T$  paths and therefore the reverse inequality. Consequently
$$\delta=B_T-F.$$
When  $\delta=0$ , every white  $T$ -terminal is also covered: its terminal path cannot start at black  $T$ , since that would be a TT path. It therefore starts at black  $H$ .The residual-count formula
$$(\mathrm{TT},\mathrm{HH},\mathrm{TH})
=
\bigl(\delta,\ b-a+\delta,\ t+2(a-\delta)\bigr)$$
then follows from the fixed numbers of selected terminals and holes.The cut normalization is valid, including untouched pathsThe terminal placements used in §2 can be reached without increasing cut capacity:Moving black  $T$  to the source side removes a unit source-arc contribution and can add at most its sole outgoing fragment.Moving white  $H$  to the sink side removes a unit sink-arc contribution and can add at most its sole incoming fragment.Moving black  $H$  to the sink side cannot add a forward crossing through an incoming fragment, because it has none.Moving white  $T$  to the source side cannot add a forward crossing through an outgoing fragment, because it has none.These arguments remain valid when the fragment directly joins two terminals. The exact two-colour cost modelThe two-colour model is exact under its stated binary shared-capacity constraints.At a switch, separate conservation of red and blue, together with two incoming and two outgoing ports, also gives conservation of the unused label. Hence the incoming and outgoing multisets of the three labels agree. One of the two allowed port bijections preserves all labels.The terminal conditions then enforce the intended endpoints:Red paths run from black  $T$  to white  $H$ .Blue paths run from black  $H$  to white  $T$ .Unused terminal paths are HH.The constraint
$$r_R+b_R\le1$$
is necessary even when  $R$  has zero  $M$ -weight: such a fragment can still own physical vertices. The note correctly uses weight only for expense, not for capacity.For the optimization equality, both directions are valid:A covering physical routing gives a feasible colouring whose objective is exactly its TH payment.A feasible colouring decodes to a covering routing whose TH payment is at most the objective.Any excess comes from coloured cycles. After decoding, such cycles are monochromatic. Erasing their colours preserves conservation and terminal conditions, including for self-loops and cycles visiting a switch through both port pairs. Since all weights are nonnegative, this cannot increase the objective.This proves equality of the two optima without an LP-integrality claim or a polynomial-time weighted-solver claim.Minor wording clarification: Equation (4) should explicitly say “when full coverage is feasible,” or adopt the convention that both infeasible minimization problems have value  $+\infty$ . Product-tile regional formula and exact debt compensationRegional cancellation counts each edge onceWith every tile available and every old tile  $N$ -copy cut, each  $P$ -fragment is one actual  $M$ -edge, directed from its white endpoint’s tile to its black endpoint’s tile. Each  $Q$ -fragment is a singleton terminal.For a tile set  $U$ , with physical union  $R$ , the normalized cut satisfies
$$B_T-\operatorname{cap}(U)
=
T_{\mathrm{black}}(R)
-H_{\mathrm{white}}(R)
-M_{\mathrm{white\text{-}out}}(R).$$
The white-vertex partition and matching partition are
$$2|U|
=
P_{\mathrm{white}}(R)+T_{\mathrm{white}}(R)+H_{\mathrm{white}}(R),$$

$$P_{\mathrm{white}}(R)
=
|M_{\mathrm{int}}(R)|+M_{\mathrm{white\text{-}out}}(R).$$
Substitution gives
$$B_T-\operatorname{cap}(U)
=
|T\cap R|+|M_{\mathrm{int}}(R)|-2|U|.$$
Here an actual edge joining two different tiles of  $U$  belongs to  $M_{\mathrm{int}}(R)$  and is counted once. An edge leaving through its white endpoint appears once in each cancelling term. An edge leaving through its black endpoint contributes to neither of those white-endpoint terms.Therefore
$$\delta_{\mathrm{tile}}
=
\max_U\left[|T\cap R|+|M_{\mathrm{int}}(R)|-2|U|\right]
=
\max_U\left[E(U)-\sum_{v\in U}s_v\right].$$
The connected-obstruction statement follows by additivity over the components of the induced actual- $M$  tile multigraph. If some region has positive excess, one component does too. Those vertices are the only possible exterior ports or terminals. Replacing the detour by their complementary tile edge preserves the connection between exactly those same ports, while isolating  $e$  as a doubled cycle.This covers all relevant cases:Either or both remaining ports are terminals.The detour lies on TT, HH, or TH.The remaining ports connect externally into the same old cycle.With two internal actual edges, all four tile vertices are internally matched by  $M$ , so there are no terminal or exterior actual-matching ports.
