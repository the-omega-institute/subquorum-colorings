The weighted witness budget is soundThe coverage criterion is exactly right. For  $x\notin S$ , coverage means either
$$a(x)\ge2,$$
or there are  $u,y\in S$  with edges  $xu,uy$ . These are precisely the possibilities in which  $x$  is the center or a leaf of a non-induced  $K_{1,2}$ .Upward closure also holds: enlarging the selection preserves every existing covering star for any vertex that remains outside. Consequently, failure of every single deletion is equivalent to inclusion-minimality.Witness existence and injectivityFor  $v\in H$ , deleting  $v$  leaves its at least two selected neighbors available. Thus the newly omitted  $v$  is covered as a center, and minimality must be witnessed by some original outside vertex  $x\in T$ .Your injectivity argument is valid, including in graphs with triangles. If  $x$  witnessed two distinct high vertices  $v,w$ , every covering star for  $x$  would have to contain both of them. Since a covering star has exactly two selected vertices, its vertex set would be  $\{x,v,w\}$ .At least one of  $v,w$ , say  $v$ , is adjacent to  $x$ . Because  $a(v)\ge2$ , there is  $y\in N(v)\cap S$  with  $y\ne w$ . Then the edges  $xv,vy$  give a covering star avoiding  $w$ , a contradiction.The non-induced convention is essential here: an additional edge  $xy$  does not invalidate this star. Thus the entire failure-witness sets of distinct high vertices are disjoint, and any choice of one witness per high vertex gives an injection.Each assigned witness pays the full weightFor  $x=f(v)$ , formation before deletion gives  $a(x)\ge1$ , while failure after deleting one vertex gives  $a(x)\le2$ .The two capacity cases are:
$$\begin{array}{ll}
xv\in E(G):
&
a(v)-1\le\Delta-2\le\Delta-a(x),\\[2mm]
xv\notin E(G):
&
a(x)=1,\qquad
a(v)-1\le\Delta-1=\Delta-a(x).
\end{array}$$
In the first case, the edge to the outside vertex  $x$  uses one of  $v$ 's degree slots. In the second, deletion does not change  $x$ 's selected-neighbor set, so two selected neighbors would keep  $x$  covered as a center.These are genuine weighted payments: one witness pays  $a(v)-1$ , which can exceed one. Injectivity ensures that the same capacity is never assigned twice.An exact slack identity verifies all the accountingFor any chosen injection, put
$$C_x=\Delta-a(x),
\qquad
q_v=C_{f(v)}-(a(v)-1).$$
The capacity argument gives  $C_x\ge0$  and  $q_v\ge0$ .The cut identity then yields the exact formula
$$\boxed{
\Delta|T|-
\left[
\sum_{v\in S}(\deg_G(v)-1)+z
\right]
=
\sum_{v\in H}q_v+
\sum_{x\in T\setminus f(H)}C_x.
}
\tag{8}$$
To see the left-hand expression, combine
$$e(S,T)=\sum_{v\in S}(\deg_G(v)-a(v))$$
with the high-vertex weights:
$$\sum_{v\in S}(\deg_G(v)-a(v))
+\sum_{v\in H}(a(v)-1)
=
\sum_{v\in S}(\deg_G(v)-1)+z.$$
A vertex with selected degree zero contributes the extra one recorded by  $z$ ; selected degree one contributes no high-vertex weight.Every term on the right of (8) is nonnegative. This proves (1), and it also shows explicitly why slack cannot cancel elsewhere in the argument.For a  $d$ -regular graph, (8) becomes
$$\boxed{
dn-(2d-1)|S|
=
z+
\sum_{v\in H}\bigl[d-a(f(v))-a(v)+1\bigr]

\sum_{x\in T\setminus f(H)}(d-a(x)).
}
