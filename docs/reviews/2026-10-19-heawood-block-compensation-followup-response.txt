The signs and the local-to-global ledger are correctFor one block  $B$ , with $s_B=|S\cap B|$ , $\delta_B=8-s_B$ , $q_B$  selected block ports, $p_B$  selected core attachment vertices, $i_B$  selected isolates in  $B$ , $W_B$  unused omitted-resource capacity in  $B$ , $E_B$  core demand paid by block resources, $I_B$  block demand paid by core resources, $F_B=E_B-I_B$ ,the incidence count
$$\sum_{v\in B}a(v)=3s_B-q_B+p_B$$
has the correct signs: a selected block port loses its deleted internal edge and contributes  $-1$ , while a selected core attachment vertex contributes the incoming  $+1$ .Consequently,
$$W_B+F_B=42-5s_B-i_B+q_B-p_B.$$
Since  $\delta_B=8-s_B$ ,
$$F_B+p_B-q_B
 =42-5s_B-i_B-W_B
 =5\delta_B+2-i_B-W_B.$$
Therefore the desired local inequality
$$F_B+p_B-q_B\le 5\delta_B+t_B$$
is exactly equivalent to
$$i_B+W_B+t_B\ge 2.$$
So the signs in the compensation argument are consistent. There is no hidden reversal of  $p_B$ ,  $q_B$ , or  $F_B$ .The global identity
$$3n-5|S|=i+W$$
then gives the result once every block contributes two units:
$$2b
 \le \sum_B(i_B+W_B+t_B)
 \le i+W
 =3n-5|S|.$$
The second inequality is legitimate because:the blocks are disjoint; $i_B$  counts selected isolates in the block; $W_B$  counts capacity on omitted block resources; $t_B$  counts selected isolated core vertices;every core vertex is incident with at most one block attachment edge.Thus no selected core isolate is charged to two blocks, and no omitted resource capacity is used twice. The witness map for high vertices is injective, so a resource cannot pay two failure demands either.The selected isolates in the closed-Heawood classification are counted in  $i$  as selected vertices. They are not omitted resources and are not also included in  $W$ .2. The closed-Heawood classification is now sufficientThe revised argument correctly uses

The clarification about  $\beta _2(H)$  resolves the earlier conditional objection. With  $\beta _2(H)=7$  meaning the maximum cardinality of a dissociation set, an eight-set containing no selected highs would itself be an eight-vertex dissociation set, contradicting  $\beta _2(H)=7$ . This does not use a minimum forming number.The degree and resource argument then works as follows.A selected leaf of a high has a private omitted resource whose selected neighborhood is the leaf, possibly together with a selected isolate.If that resource had two selected neighbors, its capacity would be only one and it could not witness deletion of the parent high; the unused capacity would contradict  $W=0$ .Therefore the leaf is supported by a one-neighbor resource.A degree-two high cannot have such a leaf, because the resulting resource capacity would leave unused capacity.A degree-three high has all three graph neighbors selected, so it has no adjacent omitted witness. Its failure witness must therefore be the third exact-classification type, namely a nonadjacent omitted vertex whose unique selected support is a selected leaf of that high.Thus every degree-three high has a leaf, and the capacity argument gives exactly one such leaf.The induced graph on selected highs is consequently two-regular.It is nonempty by the  $\beta _2(H)=7$  dissociation argument.There are exactly six selected nonisolates, so girth six forces one selected six-cycle.There are no additional leaf or matching components.Since  $W=0$ , all six omitted vertices are two-neighbor resources, each consisting of one cycle high and one selected isolate.That is the required closed-Heawood classification. The previous objection that the degree-three-high conclusion had not been forced no longer applies once the exact failure classification is explicitly invoked.One minor editorial improvement would be to state immediately before this argument:Here  $\beta _2(H)$  denotes the maximum cardinality of an induced subgraph of maximum degree at most one.That avoids any clash with alternative conventions for  $\beta _2$ .3.
