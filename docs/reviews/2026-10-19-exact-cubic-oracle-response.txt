The precise star conditionFor the standard parameter, the required  $K_{1,2}$  is not necessarily induced. This is the convention used in the primary literature defining the upper star-forming number. Thus the induced test graph on three vertices may contain a triangle and still contain the required star. combinatorialpress.comWith that convention,  $C\subseteq V(K)$  is  $2$ -star-forming precisely when every  $x\notin C$  satisfies
$$d_C(x)\ge 2
\quad\text{or}\quad
\exists u\in N_K(x)\cap C\text{ with }d_C(u)\ge1.
\tag{SF}$$
The first alternative supplies a star centered at  $x$ . The second supplies a star with  $x$  as a leaf and selected center  $u$ .This formulation makes the robustness argument especially easy to verify.Robust deletion really is safeThe preliminary claim should be stated explicitly as follows.Preservation lemma. If  $C$  is  $2$ -star-forming and  $v$  is robust for  $C$ , then  $C\setminus\{v\}$  is  $2$ -star-forming.Proof. Put  $C'=C\setminus\{v\}$ . First observe:
$$\text{Every vertex of \(C'\) that was active in \(K[C]\) remains active in \(K[C']\).}
\tag{1}$$
Indeed, a vertex not adjacent to  $v$  loses no selected neighbor. A selected neighbor of  $v$  had selected degree at least two by condition 2, so it retains another selected neighbor.Now consider vertices omitted from  $C'$ .The newly omitted  $v$ . Condition 1 supplies a selected neighbor  $u$ , and condition 2 supplies a selected neighbor of  $u$  other than  $v$ . Hence  $v$  satisfies (SF).An old omitted neighbor  $x$  of  $v$ . If  $d_C(x)\ge3$ , at least two selected neighbors remain. Otherwise condition 3 supplies its other selected neighbor  $u$ , which was active and remains active by (1).An old omitted nonneighbor  $x$  of  $v$ . Its selected neighborhood is unchanged, and every previously active selected neighbor remains active by (1). Thus its previous satisfaction of (SF) persists.This proves the lemma.  $\square$ The robustness definition itself does not require  $C$  to be star-forming. Only this preservation conclusion requires that premise. The manuscript keeps that distinction correctly.One sentence in Theorem 3 is slightly abbreviated: “its other selected core neighbor retains activity by condition 2.” Condition 2 applies directly when that neighbor is adjacent to  $v$ ; otherwise its activity is unchanged. Observation (1) is the concrete repair.2. \tag{A}$$

Consequently,
$$\frac{SF_2(F_r)-\beta_2(F_r)}{|V(F_r)|}
=
\frac{\lceil r/2\rceil}{14r}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac1{28},&r\text{ even},\\[2mm]

\end{cases}$$
