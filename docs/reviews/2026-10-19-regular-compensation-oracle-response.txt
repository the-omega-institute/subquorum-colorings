Definition and the exact covering criterionI use the direct forming condition: for every  $x\notin S$ , there are distinct  $s_1,s_2\in S$  such that the three vertices support a  $K_{1,2}$ , with extra edges permitted. This agrees with the standard definition of a  $k$ -star-forming set specialized to  $k=2$ . combinatorialpress.comWrite
$$a_S(x)=|N(x)\cap S|$$
for every vertex  $x$ , including outside vertices. Then an outside vertex  $x$  is covered exactly when
$$a_S(x)\ge2
\quad\text{or}\quad
\exists y\in N(x)\cap S\text{ with }a_S(y)\ge1. \tag{C}$$
The first alternative makes  $x$  the center; the second makes a selected neighbor  $y$  the center. Consequently, an outside vertex fails precisely when it has no selected neighbor, or has a unique selected neighbor that is isolated in the selected subgraph.This criterion validates the deletion arguments throughout the note. It also makes clear why selected isolates are allowed in a forming set: the condition is imposed on outside vertices.2. The general witness argument and exchange are correct2.1 High vertices have outside failure witnessesLet  $v\in H$ . $$N(x)\cap S=\{u\},
\qquad

Thus  $u$  is a selected leaf whose unique support is  $v$ . In particular, the witness has selected degree one.Adjacent case,  $xv\in E$ . If  $a_S(x)\ge3$ , deleting  $v$  leaves at least two selected neighbors, so failure is impossible. Hence

In the second case,  $w$  must be isolated after deleting  $v$ , so
$$N(w)\cap S\subseteq\{v\}.$$
But  $v$  and  $w$  both neighbor  $x$ , and therefore lie in the same bipartition class. They cannot be adjacent. 6. The two-connectivity proof is valid, including  $d=3$ The crown cycle can be written unambiguously as
$$a_0,b_2,a_1,b_3,\ldots,a_{d-1},b_1,a_0,$$
with indices modulo  $d$ .Its two edge types are
$$a_i b_{i+2},
\qquad
b_{i+2}a_{i+1}.$$
Their index differences are  $2$  and  $1$ , both nonzero modulo every  $d\ge3$ . It visits every  $A$ - and  $B$ -vertex exactly once. Thus the crown is Hamiltonian and two-connected.For each  $i$ ,
$$a_i-y_i-x_i-b_i$$
is an open ear: its endpoints are distinct vertices already in the crown, and its two internal vertices are new. The edge  $x_i y_i$  is present. The different ears have disjoint internal vertices, so they may be added successively.The preservation claim can be verified directly by deleting a vertex. Deleting an old vertex leaves the old graph connected, and the remaining ear vertices stay attached through at least one endpoint. Deleting an internal ear vertex leaves the two remaining ear segments attached to the connected old graph. Thus no cut vertex is introduced.After the ears, all vertices of  $X\cup Y$  are present. Adding  $p$ , with at least two distinct existing neighbors, preserves two-connectivity: after deleting any one old vertex,  $p$  retains a neighbor in the connected remainder.
