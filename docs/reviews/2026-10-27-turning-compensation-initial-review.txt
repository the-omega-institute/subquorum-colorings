Actual-edge ownership, Hall, and chargesTurning compensation note, §1.The resource identity is correct throughout: a resource is an actual edge of  $M$ , with capacity one. An internal matching edge does not acquire capacity two merely because both endpoints lie in one tile.Hall equivalenceGive tile  $B$  exactly  $d_B$  demand slots, all adjacent to the actual matching edges incident to  $B$ . Matching all slots to distinct resource vertices is precisely an ownership assignment paying every tile exactly its demand. An ownership certificate that overpays some tiles can be reduced to such an assignment by discarding excess owners.The reduction from arbitrary slot subsets to tile subsets is valid. If  $S$  is a set of slots and  $U$  its supporting tiles, then
$$|S|\le \sum_{B\in U}d_B,
\qquad
N(S)=N_M(U).$$
Thus the stated tile inequalities imply Hall’s inequality for every slot subset. Tiles with zero demand cause no problem: adding them to a tile set can only enlarge its resource neighbourhood.Conversely, an ownership certificate directly gives
$$\sum_{B\in U}d_B\le |N_M(U)|$$
because the resources owned by tiles in  $U$  are distinct incident actual edges.Ownership implies integral chargesFor each owned edge, choose one endpoint in its owner tile to receive  $+1$ , and assign  $-1$  to its other endpoint. Give both endpoints of an unused edge charge  $-1$ .A tile owning  $m_B$  resources contains exactly  $m_B$  positive endpoints, so
$$\sum_{p\in P\cap B}\sigma(p)=2m_B-p_B.$$
In particular, an owned internal edge contributes  $+1-1=0$ . This is consistent with its being one resource and having two endpoints.Since  $m_B\ge d_B\ge o_B-2$ ,
$$2m_B-p_B
\ge 2(o_B-2)-p_B
=2t_B+p_B-4
=q_B.$$
Every owned edge has charge sum zero, and every unused edge has sum  $-2$ . This establishes the required charge inequalities.Real charges imply HallFor a feasible real charge assignment, put
$$x_p=\frac{\sigma(p)+1}{2}.$$
Then  $x_p\ge0$ , and for each actual edge  $uv$ ,
$$x_u+x_v\le1.$$
The tile inequality gives
$$\sum_{p\in P\cap B}x_p
\ge \frac{q_B+p_B}{2}
=o_B-2.$$
Nonnegativity therefore improves this to
$$\sum_{p\in P\cap B}x_p\ge d_B.$$
Summing over a tile set  $U$ , each incident matching edge contributes at most one: an internal edge contributes its two endpoint weights together, while an edge leaving  $U$  contributes only one nonnegative part of its total. Consequently,
$$\sum_{B\in U}d_B
\le \sum_{p\in P\cap U}x_p
\le |N_M(U)|.$$
This proves Hall and hence integral ownership. There is no unproved geometric rounding step. The four stated conditions are equivalent.Exact resource identityAfter normalizing to exact payments, write
$$d=\sum_Bd_B,\qquad r=\sum_Br_B,\qquad b=\#\{\text{tiles}\}.$$
If  $u$  actual edges are unused, then
$$|M|=d+u.$$
Also,
$$d-r=\sum_B(o_B-2)=|T|+2|M|-2b.$$
Substituting gives
$$\boxed{
J=|T|+|M|
=2b-u-r
=\frac{\operatorname{area}}2-u-\sum_Br_B.
}$$
The exact-payment qualification matters: this formula uses the unused-edge count after excess assignments have been discarded. Under that qualification, the identity and its half-area consequence are correct.None of this algebra requires the selected-neighbour condition. That condition is used for the geometric charging lemma and feasible constructions, as the note says.2.
