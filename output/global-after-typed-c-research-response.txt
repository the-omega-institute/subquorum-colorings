The exact boundary condition for restricting original sourcesFirst, there is a general theorem describing precisely what goes wrong with a source cut. It applies to arbitrary finite mixed U/rotor fusions, with unrestricted dimensions and recurring owners.Let the complete feasible fusion have
$$P=\bigcup_iP_i,\qquad T=\left(\bigcup_iT_i\right)\setminus P.$$
For any subset  $I$  of original owners, put
$$P_I=\bigcup_{i\in I}P_i,\qquad U_I=\bigcup_{i\in I}T_i.$$
The mandatory selected set after retaining exactly those complete sources would be
$$T_I=U_I\setminus P_I.$$
Separate it into
$$V_I=U_I\setminus P,\qquad
W_I=U_I\cap(P\setminus P_I).$$
Thus
$$T_I=V_I\sqcup W_I.$$
Here  $V_I$  consists of supports that were already selected in the complete fusion. The points in  $W_I$  are restored supports: they were consumed by endpoints of deleted owners and become mandatory selections after the cut.Exact feasibility criterionEvery  $v\in V_I$  remains feasible automatically. Indeed,
$$P_I\cup V_I\cup W_I\subseteq P\cup T,$$
and  $v$  retains its original endpoint anchor in  $P_I$ .Every  $w\in W_I$  also has at least one original anchor in  $P_I$ . Full-fusion feasibility rules out any adjacency between  $V_I$  and  $W_I$ : such an adjacency would have given the globally retained selection in  $V_I$  an occupied neighbor in addition to its anchor.Consequently
$$\deg_{P_I\cup T_I}(w)
=\deg_{P_I}(w)+\deg_{W_I}(w).$$
Therefore the complete-source restriction is feasible if and only if
$$\deg_{P_I}(w)=1\quad\text{for every }w\in W_I,
\qquad E(W_I)=\varnothing.
\tag{1}$$
There are two distinct obstructions:a restored selection has two or more persistent endpoint neighbors;two restored selections are adjacent, each already having its own persistent endpoint anchor.This is why endpoint disjointness and containment of whole endpoint sets do not suffice for a recursive restriction.An exact minimum boundary omission countDefine  $\kappa(I)$  to be the minimum number of restored supports that would have to be omitted from selection to make the cut feasible, keeping  $P_I$  fixed. These omissions are forbidden in the original model. The definition measures the cut’s failure; it does not repair a fusion legally.Partition
$$W_2=\{w\in W_I:\deg_{P_I}(w)\ge2\},
\qquad W_1=W_I\setminus W_2.$$
Every point of  $W_2$  must be omitted. Once those points are removed, the remaining requirement is to eliminate every edge between selected points of  $W_1$ . Hence
$$\boxed{\kappa(I)=|W_2|+\tau\bigl(G[W_1]\bigr)
              =|W_2|+\nu\bigl(G[W_1]\bigr).}
\tag{2}$$
Here  $\tau$  is minimum vertex-cover size and  $\nu$  is maximum matching size. Their equality follows because the grid graph is bipartite.For completeness, the usual alternating-path proof gives the equality: from a maximum matching, start alternating paths at unmatched vertices in one bipartition class. The unreached vertices of that class together with the reached vertices of the other class form a vertex cover containing exactly one endpoint of every matching edge.A separate measure is total excess occupied degree:
$$\boxed{
\beta(I)
=\sum_{w\in W_I}\bigl(\deg_{P_I}(w)-1\bigr)+2|E(W_I)|.
}
\tag{3}$$
The cut is feasible exactly when  $\beta(I)=0$ .Neither  $\kappa$  nor  $\beta$  is a new reserve. The family below shows that even the optimal omission count  $\kappa$  cannot generally be paid from the existing reserve.2. What this resolves, and the precise remaining gapThe proposed spatial iteration cannot rely on either of the following unrestricted assertions:Retaining complete owners on the chosen side of a cut automatically yields another feasible complete-source fusion.The restored-support failures of such a cut can be paid individually from a uniformly bounded portion of the original  $A/U_0/Z_0/H$  pool.The first assertion fails in the complete family above. The second fails unboundedly, even after replacing a raw count of bad selections by the optimal omission count obtained from the exact boundary matching problem.The accepted restriction to whole endpoint components is unaffected. Our cut splits one endpoint component; the removed owners are precisely what consume the otherwise infeasible supports.This leaves a specific obligation for a successful general induction. It must either:carry the deleted owners’ occupied boundary bands as part of the induction state, with an accounting rule that survives further cuts and recurring owners; orprove a stronger restriction on the regions generated by actual marked-side demand that excludes this obstruction.The present family has no positive demand, so a suitably proved demand-dependent restriction could exclude it. No such restriction follows merely from complete-source containment or the existing neutral continuation rules.The pure- $01$  closed-region Hall theorem remains intact. The family realizes an open-path escape, not a closed pure- $01$  component or an AA component. U11 cap/bar continuation and complete critical-U10 realization remain separate unresolved branches.Accordingly, the full remaining claim is still
$$\boxed{2(n_2-n_0)\le A+U_0+Z_0+H.}$$
What is now established is a concrete obstruction to extending the internal-region argument by charging restored supports separately: arbitrarily many such obligations can run through full receivers belonging to the same few original owners, while the entire actual reserve stays fixed.
